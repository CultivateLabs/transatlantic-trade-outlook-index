Cultivate Labs Indices Series

Each index scores the health of a defined system on a 0 to 100 scale, against two written reference states fixed at launch. Every index publishes a pair: a direct read of conditions today calculated from published figures, and a crowd forecast of the same measures at a fixed horizon.

Current-State Index (CSI)

Calculated directly from published figures against locked buckets. No horizon, no distribution, no forecast error.

Forward-State Index (FSI)

A crowd forecast of the same drivers, as a probability distribution across buckets resolving at one fixed future date.

Spread

The forecast minus today's reading. Positive means the outlook is improving. Negative means structural pressure is building.

Methodology

One scale, two comparable readings

The current-state index maps published conditions to fixed buckets. The forward-state index uses crowd probabilities across those same buckets at one future date. Shared drivers and weights make the spread directly comparable.

Methodology

The full methodology is published alongside the scores: reference states, drivers, indicators, questions, weights, the background layer, and every change over time. Two scores are published for every index: the Current-State Index (CSI), which is today's reading, and the Forward-State Index (FSI), which is the forecast.

Reference states

These are the fixed anchors both scores are measured against. A score of 100 means conditions match the healthy state, 0 means they match the stressed state, and 50 means equidistant between them. Neither state is utopian or catastrophic.

Healthy state · 100

Barriers low, stable, and announced in advance. Treatment settled and consistently applied. Legal authority undisputed. Disputes resolved through agreed channels. Flows grow steadily. Neither side uses economic measures as leverage.

Stressed state · 0

Barriers high and revised without notice. Treatment imposed unilaterally and changed repeatedly. Legal basis contested or rebuilt mid-course. Dispute channels bypassed. Flows contract sharply or reroute. Economic measures used as leverage.

Reference states are fixed at launch and do not typically change. If they do, weights are re-evaluated and the index may need to be re-run entirely. Both the CSI and the FSI are scored against the same two states, which is what makes them directly comparable and the spread meaningful.

The scale and its bands

Both scores are published to one decimal place. A score of 50 does not mean average; it means the state sits midway between the two reference states. The score is a position on a fixed scale, not a change from a starting point.

Composite, driver, question

Each index breaks its system into a small set of structural drivers, the underlying forces that move it toward or away from the reference states. Each driver carries one or more questions. The driver-to-question relationship is one-to-many: in the Transatlantic Trade Outlook Index, five drivers carry eight questions between them.

Composite

Weighted average of driver positions, using cross-driver weights that sum to 1.00.

Driver

Weighted average of its question positions, using question weights that sum to 1.00 inside each driver.

Question

Yes/no questions, or questions with more than two possible answers. Each outcome carries a position between 0 and 100. Positions need not be evenly spaced.

For the CSI, a question's position is the bucket the current state occupies, read from published values. For the FSI, it is the probability-weighted average of its bucket positions from the forecast. Both legs use the same aggregation and the same weights. If a question has to be dropped, its share is spread across the other questions in that driver.

Weights

Weights are derived at launch rather than defaulted to equal. The preferred path is derivation by the analytical team with expert review. Where that capacity is not available, or as a reviewable first pass, an initial set may be drafted and then reviewed, edited and ratified by a named human; the path used is recorded in the Weight Derivation Note. Weights are not adjusted in response to short-term score movements.

The current index uses five cross-driver weights and a documented within-driver split wherever a driver contains more than one question. The same weights apply to the current and forward readings.

The background layer

The background layer holds forecast questions that matter for interpretation but never enter the index. They are never weighted or summed into today's reading, the forecast or the spread. They reach the product two other ways: forecasters use them when answering the scored questions, and readers use them to make sense of the numbers. A healthy-looking index sitting next to a rising disruption risk is exactly what the layer is there to surface.

Validation and track record

The two legs are asymmetric, and the disclosure matters. Only the FSI is a forecast, so only the FSI accumulates a calibration track record: at question level, how closely its bucket probabilities matched what actually happened; at composite level, the FSI error series against the realized CSI. The CSI is calculated directly from published figures, so there is nothing to forecast and nothing to get wrong.

Today's FSI is the expected value of the CSI that will be read at the horizon, not a point prediction. A single realized CSI differing from the FSI is not evidence of miscalibration; only the distribution of errors over many resolutions is informative. Spread validation is published as a monthly numeric series at composite and driver level, reporting mean error, and comparing the forecast against simply assuming nothing changes.
